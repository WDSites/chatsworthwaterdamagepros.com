Water removal is the extraction of standing and surface water from a building using pumps, extraction units and wet vacuums. It is the first mechanical step in any water loss. Drying the structure follows, and that is covered under water mitigation.

When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is urgent the moment it is deeper than a puddle or sits on carpet. Pooled water wicks up drywall, soaks padding and seeps under flooring, and it keeps doing so until it is pulled out. Waiting until morning often means a bigger repair bill.

Same-day response is offered when available. After-hours callers reach the same dispatch as daytime callers, and the dispatcher will ask about depth, source and electrical exposure before sending a crew.

Safety first. Do not step into standing water if outlets, power strips or appliances are submerged. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line. If the water came from a drain or toilet, treat it as sewage and stay out.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe can release a large volume of water in minutes. The first move is the main valve, usually near the front of the house or at the water heater line. If it will not close, call LADWP, the water utility, to shut off supply at the meter.

After shutoff, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line and relieve pressure. Photograph the break and the damage before cleanup, and keep any broken pipe section for your adjuster. A licensed plumber repairs the pipe itself, while the restoration crew deals with the water and the drying.

Hidden bursts are harder. A pipe inside a wall or under a slab may leak for hours before a stain shows up. Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters help trace the wet path so only the necessary drywall is opened.

Most policies treat a sudden burst differently from a slow drip. An abrupt, accidental loss is often covered while long-term seepage often is not, so check your policy. Make temporary repairs to prevent further damage, and keep receipts.

What happens after the water is out

Extraction removes the bulk, but not the moisture that has already moved into carpet backing, drywall and framing. That is why drying equipment usually follows, with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers working until moisture meters read dry. Skipping this step is how a musty smell shows up a week later.

Take it out or leave it in: the trade-offs

Pumping works fast but cannot fix a leak that is still running. Extraction alone also cannot rescue swollen particleboard or sagging ceilings. Sometimes the honest answer is that padding comes out the same day, because it holds water against the carpet and slows drying.

  • Clean supply water caught quickly gives you the best chance of saving flooring
  • Water that sat overnight shifts the odds toward removing padding and baseboards
  • Anything from a drain, toilet or outside is handled as contaminated

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water removal emergencies at night or on weekends in Chatsworth?

Yes. The line runs around the clock, including weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, but arrival times cannot be promised since they depend on where crews are. Shut off the water, stay clear of electrical hazards and call.

How quickly does standing water damage my floors?

Damage starts as soon as water reaches porous material. Padding and drywall absorb moisture within hours, and wood floors begin to cup after longer exposure. Faster extraction usually means fewer materials to replace.

A pipe burst inside the wall and I cannot see the water. What now?

Shut off the main valve, then call. Stains or soft spots may be the only sign. The crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find the wet area and opens only the drywall that needs it, then you can bring in a plumber for the pipe.

Can water be removed from carpet without throwing it out?

Often yes, if the water was clean and extraction began quickly. The padding is commonly replaced, since it holds water and dries slowly. Carpet that sat in contaminated water is normally discarded, and our water damage cleanup page explains why.

Do I need to move furniture before the crew arrives?

Lift small items and electronics onto dry surfaces if it is safe to reach them. Leave heavy furniture to the crew. Do not stand in water to move anything near outlets.

Does water removal include fixing the burst pipe?

No. Removal deals with water, and plumbing repair is a separate trade. A licensed plumber fixes the pipe, and later rebuilding work is described on the water damage repair page.
