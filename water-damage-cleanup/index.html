Water damage cleanup is the removal of water, debris and ruined materials from a building, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and deodorizing the surfaces that stay. It starts once the source is stopped, and it overlaps with water mitigation, which handles the drying side.

Why the first 48 hours of cleanup are urgent

Wet carpet padding, cardboard and drywall edges start to sour fast. Bacteria multiply in porous material that stays wet, and the odor spreads through the house on the air. Early removal costs less labor than cleaning up after a smell has set into wood and fabric.

That is why calls are treated as urgent from the first ring. Same-day response is offered when available, and after-hours calls go to the same dispatch as daytime ones. Nobody can promise an arrival time, but the sooner the call, the more you can save.

Safety first. Stay out of any water that touches outlets, appliances or the panel. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line. If sewage is in the water, keep everyone out of it.

How a cleanup job runs

  1. Walk the home, confirm the water source is off, and check for electrical and sewage risks.
  2. Put on protective gear and pull out wet debris, ruined padding and swollen trim.
  3. Extract what remains with wet vacuums and extraction units.
  4. Cut out drywall or flooring that cannot be dried, bagging and hauling it away.
  5. Scrub and wipe surfaces, then apply antimicrobial treatment to the framing and floors that stay.
  6. Run HEPA air scrubbers to clear dust and airborne particles from the demolition.
  7. Check the area with a moisture meter before the drying equipment goes in.

Sanitizing and deodorizing: what actually removes the smell

Spray fragrance covers an odor for an afternoon. The smell comes from wet residue and damp porous material, so it only goes away when those are removed and the surfaces are cleaned. Antimicrobial treatment handles bacteria on the surfaces that remain.

Air scrubbers pull odor-carrying particles out of the air while the structure dries. If a smell returns after cleanup, something is usually still wet behind a wall or under flooring. A moisture meter or thermal imaging camera can find it.

Furniture, clothing and papers: what to keep

Solid wood furniture and hard plastic items usually clean up well. Mattresses, upholstered pieces and particleboard shelves soak water deeply and often cannot be saved, especially after contaminated water. Documents and photos can sometimes be frozen and dried later, so do not toss them in a panic.

Photograph each damaged item and write down its brand and model before it leaves the house. The California Department of Insurance says not to discard damaged items until the adjuster says it is acceptable, so ask first and keep a log of calls.

Insurance basics for cleanup

A one-time accidental event is often covered, while slow leaks often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate coverage, so check your policy. Keep receipts for temporary protection steps, because the state guide says insurers reimburse reasonable costs to protect property, and ask your insurer before any permanent repair.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between water damage cleanup and water removal?

Water removal pumps and extracts the standing water. Cleanup is the larger job that follows, including debris removal, cutting out ruined materials, and sanitizing. Often both happen on the same visit, and the emergency water removal page explains the pumping side.

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Chatsworth?

Yes, the line is staffed overnight and through weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, but no one can honestly promise an exact arrival time. Tell the dispatcher what the water touched, because that decides which protective steps the crew needs to plan.

The house still smells musty after I mopped. Why?

Mopping cleans the surface, but the odor usually comes from moisture trapped in padding, baseboards or wall cavities. A technician traces it with a moisture meter, removes what cannot dry, and treats what remains. Persistent dampness can also lead to mold, which is a separate service.

Can I clean up after water damage myself?

For a small spill of clean water, yes. For anything that touched sewage, soaked drywall, or spread across several rooms, professional gear and extraction units do the job better and more safely. If you do start, wear gloves and boots, and keep photos of everything.

Will the carpet be saved or thrown out?

It depends on the water source and how long it sat. Carpet that got clean water and is caught early can often be dried in place, while the padding is commonly replaced. Carpet touched by sewage or storm water is normally removed.

Do you haul away the debris?

Yes, ruined materials are bagged and taken out as part of the job. Tell the dispatcher about large items like soaked mattresses or cabinets so the crew can plan for them. Keep items an adjuster needs to see until your insurer says otherwise.
