What to tell dispatch

Dispatch asks a handful of questions, and clear answers get the right crew and equipment moving. You do not need technical words. Say what you see.

  • Is the source stopped? Say whether you shut off the water or the leak is still running.
  • Which rooms are affected, and is water on floors, walls, ceilings or all three?
  • How long has it been wet? Minutes, hours or days changes the plan.
  • What kind of water is it? Clean supply water, gray water from a washer or dishwasher, or sewage.
  • Is power on in the wet area, and have any outlets, lights or appliances been touched by water?
  • Your address, your ZIP and the nearest cross street.

If water is touching outlets, the panel or appliances, stay out of the area. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. For sparks, gas or any electrical risk, call 911 or the utility before calling us.

Why there is no online form

This site has no contact form, and that is on purpose. When water is moving, every hour of waiting lets it spread into subfloors, wall cavities and insulation.

A call lets dispatch ask follow-up questions, catch safety problems and decide what equipment to send while you are still on the line. A form sits in an inbox until someone reads it. For this kind of problem, a conversation is faster.

What to have ready for insurance

The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners to report damage right away, keep a log of calls and copy all correspondence. It also says to photograph the damage and list damaged items with brand and model where you can.

Do not throw out damaged items until your adjuster says it is acceptable. Keep receipts for temporary steps you take to protect the property, since your policy may reimburse reasonable costs to do so. Check your policy, and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

Homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage. Flood coverage is a separate policy, so check yours before you assume anything.

While you wait for the crew

Small actions help. If it is safe, stop the source, move small items and rugs out of the water, and lift furniture legs off wet carpet. Take photos and short videos before moving things.

Do not run a household vacuum on standing water, and do not walk through sewage water. If the water came from a toilet or drain backup, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets out.

Frequently asked questions

Can I request service by email?

Email exists for follow-up, but it is not a way to request emergency service. Water damage gets worse by the hour, and a ringing phone gets answered faster than an inbox. Call the dispatch line and describe the situation, then use email afterward to send photos or documents.

Is dispatch really open 24/7?

Yes. Dispatch takes calls at any hour, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher will tell you what the next step looks like when you call.

What if I do not know what kind of water it is?

Say so. Tell dispatch where the water came from and what it looks like. Water from a toilet, drain or outside flooding should be treated as contaminated. Keep people and pets out, and the crew will assess the category on site.

Do I need to call my insurance company first?

Not before you call about an active leak. Stop the source if it is safe, call dispatch, and report the loss to your insurer promptly. The California Department of Insurance says to report damage immediately. Check your policy for how and when to file.
