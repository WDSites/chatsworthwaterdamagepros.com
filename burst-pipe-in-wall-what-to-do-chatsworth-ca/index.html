Burst Pipe in Wall What to Do Chatsworth, CA

The first few actions are about stopping the flow, staying safe and recording what happened.

☎ Call (818) 337-2642 now

By the Chatsworth Water Damage Pros team · Updated October 2026

You hear a hiss inside the wall, or a stain spreads across the drywall, or water simply starts running down from the baseboard. Whatever the first sign was, a burst pipe in a wall is an emergency, and the order of your next moves matters.

This guide gives you a sequence for the first stretch of the event, what to photograph, how to spot a leak you have not seen yet, and when to bring in professionals. It is written for a homeowner standing in a wet hallway, so it starts with the main shutoff.

Know where your water shuts off before you need it

Most homes have two places to stop water. One is a main shutoff valve at the house, often near where the supply line enters, by the water heater or in a utility area. The other is the water meter near the street, which belongs to the utility.

Find the house valve today, even if nothing is leaking. Turn it by hand to confirm it moves, and tag it so anyone in the family can find it. A valve that has not moved in years can be stuck, and you want to learn that on a dry day.

  • Interior main shutoff, usually a round wheel handle or a lever.
  • Meter box at the curb, which may need a meter key and is the utility's equipment.

The first moves, in order

These are a sequence, not a schedule. Do each step before moving to the next, and skip nothing that applies to your situation.

  1. Shut off the main valve at the house. Turn clockwise for a wheel, or a quarter turn so the lever sits crosswise to the pipe.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain what remains in the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. Look at the floor around you before stepping closer to the wall. If water is near outlets, the panel, appliances or power strips, treat it as an electrical risk.
  4. If the electrical panel is dry and safe to reach, switch off the breakers for the affected area. If the panel is wet or you would have to stand in water, do not touch it.
  5. Call 911 or the utility if you smell gas, see sparks, or the situation is unsafe. Do that before calling a restoration line.
  6. Move valuables, rugs and small furniture out of the wet zone, and lift anything that can wick up water.
  7. Photograph and video the damage, then call a water removal crew.

Water and electricity do not mix. Do not stand in standing water near outlets or the breaker panel. If in doubt, stay out and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk.

If the main valve will not turn

A stuck or broken house valve means water keeps flowing. Do not force it until the stem snaps. The meter shutoff becomes your next option.

Contact the water utility. For Chatsworth that is LADWP, and it is the right call for meter problems or a failure in the service line between the meter and your house. Ask LADWP what it can do and who is responsible for which part of the line. A plumber can then replace the failed house valve.

What to photograph and write down

Good records protect you later when you talk to your insurer. The California Department of Insurance says to report damage immediately, photograph it and list damaged items with brand and model. It also says to keep a log of calls and copy all correspondence.

Take wide shots of each room first, then close-ups of the wall, the floor and anything the water touched. Photograph the valve you closed. Do not throw away damaged items until the adjuster says that is acceptable.

  • The wet wall, with a ruler or hand for scale.
  • Ceiling stains in rooms below.
  • Baseboards, flooring and carpet edges.

Signs you may have a hidden leak

Not every pipe fails loudly. A slow leak inside a wall can run for a long time before anyone notices, and by then the drywall, insulation and framing can be wet.

Why speed in drying matters

Water spreads along framing, soaks into insulation and wicks up drywall far past the visible stain. The longer materials stay wet, the greater the chance of mold. The CDI guide says to dry wet areas promptly with air circulation and dehumidification after sudden water damage.

A crew from emergency water removal uses extraction units to pull standing water, then moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find damp spots behind walls. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what is left. Ask whether the work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

The repair, the drying and the order they happen

A plumber fixes the pipe, and the restoration crew dries and removes damaged materials. Often a section of drywall is opened so the cavity can dry properly. That can feel drastic, but sealed wet wall material tends to stay wet.

A full water mitigation plan covers stopping the loss, drying and documenting readings for the adjuster. Make temporary protective repairs and keep receipts. The CDI advises against extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster has assessed the damage.

Frequently asked questions

Should I turn off my electricity if a pipe burst in the wall?

Only if the electrical panel is dry and safe to reach. Never stand in water to get to the panel. If water is reaching outlets, the panel or appliances, stay out of the area and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk before calling a restoration company. Your electrician can check the circuits once the area is dry.

Who do I call in Chatsworth if the leak is at the water meter?

The water utility for Chatsworth is LADWP. Call LADWP for meter problems or a service-line failure, and ask which parts of the line are the utility's and which are yours. For a failed pipe inside the house, a licensed plumber handles the repair, and a water removal crew handles drying.

Do I need to open the wall after a pipe burst?

Often yes. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water traveled, and if the cavity is wet, drying it in place can be slow or incomplete. Opening a section lets air movers and dehumidifiers reach the wet material. The crew should explain what they found and what they propose to remove.

Will my homeowners policy cover a burst pipe?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Report the damage immediately, take reasonable steps to protect the property and keep your receipts. Policies may not cover ensuing damage if you did not take reasonable steps to secure the home.

Is a leak in a Chatsworth home harder to spot in the dry months?

It can be. Rain falls mostly November through March and summers are nearly dry, so a stain that appears in July cannot be blamed on the roof. Look at plumbing first. Check the meter with every fixture off, feel for warm or damp wall sections, and call for a moisture check if anything seems off.
