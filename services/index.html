The six services

Water mitigation is the first-response step. Crews stop further loss by controlling the source, removing water and setting drying equipment. Starting mitigation early is what limits how much of the building gets wet.

Water damage cleanup covers cleaning and sanitizing after water is out. It removes soaked materials, treats surfaces and deals with contamination left behind.

Water damage repair is the rebuild phase. After materials are dry, drywall, flooring, insulation and finishes are replaced. Permits and inspections may apply, so ask the city.

Flood damage restoration covers larger events where water enters from outside, such as storm runoff. These losses often involve contaminated water and need a stricter process.

Emergency water removal is extraction with pumps and extraction units when standing water is on the floor now.

Mold remediation handles mold growth, using containment, HEPA air scrubbers and removal of affected material, following ANSI/IICRC S520.

The order of work from stop to rebuild

Good jobs follow a sequence. Skipping a step usually costs more later, since hidden moisture can lead to mold and repeat repairs.

  1. Stop the source. Shut off the water if it is safe, or call a licensed plumber for the fix.
  2. Call dispatch and describe the loss, so the right crew and equipment head your way.
  3. Remove standing water with pumps and extraction units.
  4. Find hidden moisture with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until readings return to normal.
  6. Clean and treat surfaces, including antimicrobial treatment where needed.
  7. Rebuild damaged materials once the structure is confirmed dry.

Standards behind the work

The published industry standard for water damage is ANSI/IICRC S500, and for mold it is ANSI/IICRC S520. They guide how water is classified, how drying is documented and how mold is contained.

Ask any company whether its crews work to these standards. A good answer describes what gets measured on your job and when drying is considered done.

Water, insurance and who decides what

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

The California Department of Insurance advises making temporary repairs to prevent further damage and keeping receipts. It also says not to make extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster has assessed the damage.

We do not give insurance advice, but we can explain what we found and what we did. Questions about scope or pricing are answered by phone and on site before work starts.

Frequently asked questions

Which service should I call for first?

If water is standing now, ask for emergency water removal. If water is gone but materials are damp, ask for water mitigation. If you see growth, ask for mold remediation. Dispatch sorts it out on the call, so describe what you see and let them point you.

Do I need mold remediation after every leak?

No. Mold needs moisture, and fast drying often prevents it. The California Department of Insurance notes that prompt drying with air circulation and dehumidification limits mold after sudden water damage. If a leak ran for a long time, mold may already be present and testing is advised.

Does the same crew do repair and mitigation?

Mitigation and repair are different phases. Mitigation stops the loss and dries the structure. Repair rebuilds what was removed. Dispatch can explain how the phases connect on your job, and you agree to the plan for each before work starts.

Are S500 and S520 required by law?

They are published industry standards, not statutes. They describe how professionals approach water damage and mold. Ask any company whether its crews work to them and what that means in practice. Permits and code questions go to the City of Los Angeles.

How are prices set?

We do not post prices because every loss differs. The scope is explained by phone and on site before work starts, and you agree to the plan first. If hidden damage changes the plan, you hear about it before the scope grows.
